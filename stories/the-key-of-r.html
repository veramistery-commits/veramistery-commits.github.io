9 November 1965

The Key of R

On November 9, 1965, at 5:16 p.m., the lights of the Northeast went out. Thirty million people, 80,000 square miles, up to thirteen hours.

One protective relay at the Sir Adam Beck station in Ontario had been misprogrammed. On a cold evening near peak load it tripped, exactly as instructed. The load jumped to the neighbouring lines, theirs tripped too, and the failure walked east through the grid in under five minutes, island by island.

Nobody switched anything off. Every piece did what it was told.

Minutes earlier, a DJ in New York, Dan Ingram, noticed the record he was playing sounded slow. It came out in what he called the key of R. The turntables ran on motors timed by the 60-cycle current, and the current was already at 56 cycles, then 51. He told his listeners the electricity itself was slowing down, and added: "I didn't know that could happen."

Most phones worked. 800,000 people were stuck in the subway. Looting reports: five.

Sixty-one years later I keep the part I can use. A system doesn't only fail by breaking. Sometimes it fails by obeying, exactly, the wrong line, and sounding normal while the cycles fall.
